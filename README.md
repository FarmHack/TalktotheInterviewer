# Talk to the…

A recorder for long semi-structured interviews. One file, no server, no install,
no account. Open `index.html` in a browser and it works — on a laptop with no
network, on a plane, in a field.

**Try it:** https://farmhack.github.io/talk-to-the/

## What it does

It records **once**, straight through, and marks the moment each question was
asked, so a ninety-minute recording can be read back question by question
afterwards instead of scrubbed through.

- One question at a time, with the next one always in view
- Big record and pause; a level meter that moves, so a dead microphone is visible
- A place to write what they said while they are still saying it
- Feedback on the questions themselves — the question set is meant to improve
- Question sets built in, in every language they have been translated into

## What it does with the recording

**Nothing you did not ask it to.** There is no server here. When the interview
ends you are handed two files — the recording, and a sidecar holding the consent
you recorded, your marks and your notes. They stay in the browser until you save
them, and the browser's copy is deleted only when you say so.

If someone has given you a **one-time code** for a Farm Hack box, you can send
the interview to that box directly and it will answer with a receipt: the
checksum of the audio it stored, the length it measured against the length you
recorded, how many of your marks found their question, and whether it sealed and
verified. Only then does it say your copy is a second copy rather than the only
one.

## The recording is the record

The original audio and the exact questions it was recorded against are sealed
together as one package, read-only, before anything is derived from it. Every
transcript, every analysis, every quote comes from that pair. If a participant
withdraws, the package is destroyed and a tombstone keeps the checksums and the
reason — a withdrawal you cannot demonstrate having honoured is worth very
little to the person who asked for it.

## Consent

The consent screen is not a formality and cannot be skipped. It asks, in plain
words, what will be recorded, where it will be processed, who may hear it and
what may be quoted, and it will not start the interview until it has been read.
Participant-facing consent text is shown only in languages a speaker has
reviewed — a machine-drafted translation is fine for finding your way around the
tool and is not informed consent.

## Privacy

This page makes no requests to anyone. It loads no fonts, no analytics, no
libraries, no tiles. Everything it needs is in the file. You can read it — it is
not minified — and you can check with your browser's network tab, which should
stay empty for the whole interview.
